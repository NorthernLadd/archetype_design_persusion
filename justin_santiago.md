# Visual Direction

Because the brand archetype is **Hero + Explorer**, the visual language should feel:

- Technical
- Athletic
- Rugged
- Confident
- Minimal
- Performance-focused

Avoid making the brand look like a generic luxury clothing company. The product should feel like **athletic equipment**, not simply casual clothing.

## Imagery

Use imagery such as:

- Athletes training in demanding environments
- Outdoor workouts
- Running, lifting, and sports
- Close-ups of fabric and stitching
- Fabric durability testing
- Stretch and seam testing
- Sweat and weather conditions
- Athletes continuing to perform despite difficult conditions
- Groups of athletes training together

The goal is to communicate:

> **This shirt is equipment for performance.**

---

# Color Palette

A strong starting palette:

- **White** — cleanliness, simplicity, product focus
- **Black** — strength and confidence
- **Charcoal** — technical and industrial feeling
- **Steel Gray** — engineering and durability
- **One accent color** — orange, electric blue, or red for energy

### Suggested hierarchy

**White → Black → Charcoal → Steel → Athletic Accent**

Keep the palette restrained. Because the product itself is a **white T-shirt**, the brand should allow the white product to remain visually important.

---

# Font Direction

Use a **bold, modern sans-serif** for the primary identity.

## Option A — Performance

- Headings: **Montserrat ExtraBold**
- Body: **Inter**
- Numbers/specifications: **Roboto Mono**

This combination communicates athleticism while remaining modern and approachable.

## Option B — Technical

- Headings: **Oswald Bold**
- Body: **Inter**
- Technical information: **IBM Plex Mono**

This creates a more engineered and industrial appearance.

## Option C — Minimal

- Headings: **Helvetica/Arial-style bold sans**
- Body: **Inter**

This creates a clean, premium, minimalist appearance.

# Cialdini Principle: Authority

One of the strongest opportunities for the brand is proving that the product is actually durable.

The problem is that almost every clothing company can claim:

> "Our shirts are extremely durable."

Instead, show the evidence.

## Possible visual system

**50 WASHES**
↓
**FABRIC STRENGTH TEST**
↓
**STRETCH TEST**
↓
**SEAM TEST**
↓
**REAL ATHLETE TRAINING**

Then:

> **WE DON'T ASK YOU TO TRUST THE CLAIM. WE TEST IT.**

This combines the brand's **Sage** and **Hero** characteristics.

# Recommended Cialdini Priorities

Not every persuasion principle needs to be equally prominent.

For this brand, the strongest fit is:

1. **Authority** — Prove the engineering and durability.
2. **Social Proof** — Show real athletes using the product.
3. **Commitment & Consistency** — Connect the shirt to consistent training.
4. **Unity** — Build an identity around people who push themselves.
5. **Reciprocity** — Provide useful training and product knowledge.

## Be Careful With Scarcity

Scarcity can work, but it should not conflict with the brand's trust-focused identity.

For example, constantly using:

> **"ONLY 50 LEFT!!! BUY NOW!!!"**

could feel inconsistent with a brand positioned around expertise, reliability, and authenticity.

Scarcity should therefore be used selectively, such as for:

- Limited editions
- Numbered releases
- Special collaborations
- Seasonal drops
